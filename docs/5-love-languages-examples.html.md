## The 5 love languages with everyday examples

By [Jack Wallner](https://jackwallner.com/) · Last updated October 28, 2026

The five love languages are words of affirmation, quality time, receiving gifts, acts of service and physical touch, a model from counselor Gary Chapman. Each is a way of showing care, and most gestures take under a minute: a specific compliment, ten phone-free minutes, a small gift, a chore done unasked, a long hug. Research suggests people appreciate all five, so a mix works better than guessing a single one.

## Where do the five love languages come from?

Gary Chapman, a marriage counselor and author, introduced the five love languages in his 1992 book *The Five Love Languages*. His premise, as his own site puts it, is that different people give and receive love in different ways, and that learning to recognize those preferences helps you connect. The five languages are below, each with a short description based on Chapman's wording and some everyday examples. The examples are my suggestions, not Chapman's.

## What are words of affirmation?

Words of affirmation means using words to affirm other people, in Chapman's description.

- Name one specific thing: "I liked how you handled that call with your sister."
- Send a text in the middle of the workday that says why you are glad you married or chose them.
- Thank them for a routine job: "Thanks for doing the school pickup again."
- Leave a short note in their bag, on the mirror or in their lunch.
- Tell someone else something good about them while they are in the room.

## What does quality time mean?

Quality time means giving the other person your undivided attention, according to Chapman's site.

- Ten minutes at the end of the day with phones in another room.
- A walk around the block after dinner, without a podcast.
- Cook one meal together every week and talk while you do it.
- Ask a question that needs more than a yes or no, then finish listening before you answer.
- Put a standing date on the calendar, even if it is breakfast at home.

## What does receiving gifts mean?

Receiving gifts is the language where a heartfelt gift is what makes some people feel most loved, as Chapman's site describes it. The thought matters more than the price.

- Pick up their usual coffee order or a favorite snack on the way home.
- Bring back something small from a trip that shows you were thinking of them.
- Give a flower from the grocery store, or a book you think they would like.
- Save a ticket stub or a photo from a good day and give it back to them.
- Wrap something ordinary, like a new pair of socks, because you noticed theirs wore out.

## What are acts of service?

Acts of service means doing helpful things for your partner. Chapman's phrase for this language is that actions speak louder than words.

- Do the chore that is theirs this week, without mentioning it.
- Fill their car with gas or run the errand they have been putting off.
- Make the phone call they dread, such as the insurance company or the dentist.
- Have breakfast or coffee ready before they are out of bed.
- Take over bedtime or the dishes on the night they are worn out.

## What does physical touch mean as a love language?

Physical touch is the language where nothing speaks more deeply than appropriate physical touch, as Chapman puts it. Appropriate means welcome: ask or watch how your partner responds, and respect no.

- A long hug when you walk in the door.
- Holding hands while walking or in the car.
- A hand on the back while they cook or work.
- Sitting close enough on the couch to touch.
- A foot rub or shoulder rub during a stressful week.

## Are love languages backed by research?

Not well. A 2023 review by Emily Impett and colleagues in *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, summarized by the Association for Psychological Science, found that research does not support the basic claims of love languages. People like and appreciate all five ways of receiving love, not only one. The five categories overlap considerably. And researchers have not found a consistent link between relationship satisfaction and either matching or mismatching on languages. All expressions of love, regardless of a person's stated primary language, were related to higher satisfaction.

The authors suggest thinking of love as a balanced diet instead: people need a mix of several relational nutrients over time. For this guide, that means the five categories are useful as a checklist of ways to show care, and you do not need to work out your partner's single language before you start. A habit that rotates through all five covers everything.

## How do you remember to do small gestures?

Put them on a rotation. The hard part is rarely knowing what to do. It is remembering to do it in a week full of work and errands. A rotation helps: choose one language per day or per week, pick one gesture from the lists above, and do it before the day ends. Keep the gestures small enough that you will actually do them.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the 5 love languages?

Gary Chapman's five love languages are words of affirmation, quality time, receiving gifts, acts of service and physical touch. Each describes a way of showing and feeling care.

### What are examples of acts of service?

Doing a chore your partner usually does, filling their gas tank, making a phone call they dread, having breakfast ready, or taking over bedtime on a night they are worn out. They work best when they are done without being asked.

### What are examples of quality time?

Ten phone-free minutes at the end of the day, a walk after dinner, cooking one meal together each week, or a standing date on the calendar. Chapman's site describes this language as giving the other person your undivided attention.

### Do I need to know my partner's love language first?

Not necessarily. A 2023 review summarized by the Association for Psychological Science found that people like all five ways of receiving love, and matching a partner's main language did not consistently predict satisfaction. Rotating through all five is a practical approach.

### Does my partner need the app for Little Gestures to work?

No. You can create and use your own reminders without your partner installing anything. Shared milestones and the daily check-in need your partner to pair with you through an invite link.
